By the time the decision lands on Thursday afternoon, the suspense will largely be
theatre. Futures markets put the odds of no change at well above ninety percent.
What investors are really trading is not the rate but the *tone*.

## The number nobody is watching

A hold is a hold. The interesting question is the word that follows it: whether the
statement keeps the door open to a cut later this year, or quietly closes it.

| Scenario      | Market reaction (expected) |
| ------------- | -------------------------- |
| Dovish hold   | Equities up, yields down   |
| Neutral hold  | Muted, range-bound         |
| Hawkish hold  | Sell-off in rate-sensitive |

Analysts have spent the week parsing speeches for adjectives. One word — *patient*
versus *vigilant* — can move billions.

## The longer arc

Strip away the noise and the picture is one of an economy that refuses to behave
predictably. Inflation has cooled but not vanished. The labour market is soft at
the edges and rigid in the middle. Every model that confidently called the last
cycle is hedging now.

> "We are data-dependent," the chair likes to say, which is another way of
> admitting that nobody is steering with a map anymore.

For ordinary savers, the takeaway is less dramatic than the headlines suggest:
rates are high, they will stay high for a while, and the cost of borrowing is now
a feature of the landscape rather than a passing storm.
