I went in prepared to be bored. I came out arguing with strangers in the lobby.
This is, by some distance, the best thing this franchise has produced — and almost
nobody is going to see it.

## The case against, made first

Let us be fair to the skeptics, because I was one. The previous two films were
loud, expensive and empty. The studio had wrung the premise dry. A fourth entry
read like a contractual obligation.

> The miracle of this film is that it behaves as if it has nothing to prove, and
> therefore proves everything.

## What changed

A new director, working with a fraction of the usual budget, did the unthinkable:
they made the film *small*. The world-ending stakes are gone. In their place is a
quiet story about two people and a long drive.

The action sequences — when they come — land harder precisely because the film
spent two acts making you care.

### Three things it gets right

1. **Restraint.** It trusts silence.
2. **Faces.** The camera stays on the actors long enough to let them act.
3. **An ending that commits.** No sequel hook. No mid-credits wink. It simply
   ends, and means it.

## Go see it

It will not top the box office. It will not spawn a cinematic universe. It is just
a very good film, made by people who clearly loved making it, dropped into a market
that no longer knows what to do with that. Go anyway. Argue with a stranger in the
lobby afterward. That is what films are for.
