The pitch deck was beautiful. The market was, the founders insisted, enormous. The
only thing the company never quite managed to find was a customer willing to pay.

## A failure with no villain

It is tempting to look for fraud, for hubris, for some dramatic flaw. There was
none. The founders were earnest. The engineers were good. The investors were
sophisticated. And the company still burned through its funding without booking
meaningful revenue.

> "Everyone was waiting for product-market fit to arrive," a former employee said.
> "Like it was a train. It never came."

### The slow tells

1. **Pilots that never converted.** Customers loved trying the product for free
   and vanished when the invoice arrived.
2. **A roadmap driven by demos, not deployments.** The team built what looked
   impressive, not what got used.
3. **Funding as a substitute for revenue.** Each round bought another year of not
   answering the only question that mattered.

## The autopsy

The company's collapse was quiet. There was no scandal, just a final email, a
skeleton crew winding down servers, and a Slack workspace that went still.

The lesson is unglamorous and worth repeating: a great product nobody pays for is
not a business. It is a very expensive hobby with a logo.
