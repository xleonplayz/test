The modern newsroom is optimized for a single metric it should distrust:
*velocity*. The first outlet to publish captures the clicks, the social shares,
the algorithmic boost. The second outlet, even if more accurate, inherits the
crumbs.

This is a bad trade, and we should say so plainly.

## Speed is not a virtue

Being fast is a skill. It is not a value. A newsroom that confuses the two will,
sooner or later, publish something fast and wrong — and the correction, posted
quietly hours later, will never travel as far as the error.

> The retraction is always smaller than the headline. That asymmetry is the whole
> problem.

## What slow actually means

Slow journalism is not lazy journalism. It is journalism that:

1. Calls the second source before publishing, not after.
2. Treats "we don't know yet" as a publishable sentence.
3. Measures success in trust accrued, not attention rented.

We are not nostalgic for a golden age that never existed. Old newsrooms made
plenty of fast, confident mistakes. But the tools have changed, and the pressure
has multiplied, and the cost of being wrong has been quietly socialized onto the
reader.

## The wager

The wager of a slow newsroom is simple: that over a long enough horizon, readers
reward the outlet that was *right* more than the one that was *first*. We are
betting our institution on it. We think it is the only bet worth making.
